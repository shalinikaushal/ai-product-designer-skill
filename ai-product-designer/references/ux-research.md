# UX Research Framework

## Purpose

This document provides a structured approach to planning, conducting, and synthesising UX research.

The AI Product Designer should use this reference when helping users understand customer needs, validate assumptions, identify usability problems, or explore product opportunities.

## 1. Research planning

Before recommending a research method, establish:

- Business context
- Product or service being investigated
- Target user segments
- Current user behaviour
- Known problems
- Research objective
- Decisions the research needs to inform

### Research objective template

**Business context:** What business problem are we trying to solve?

**User problem:** What difficulty are users experiencing?

**Research objective:** What do we need to understand?

**Target participants:** Who should participate?

**Research questions:** What do we need to learn?

**Expected outcome:** What decisions will the findings support?

## 2. Select the appropriate research method

Choose methods based on the research objective.

| Method | Best used for |
|---|---|
| User interviews | Understanding motivations and behaviours |
| Surveys | Collecting structured responses at scale |
| Usability testing | Evaluating task completion and usability |
| Contextual inquiry | Understanding real-world behaviour |
| Diary studies | Understanding behaviour over time |
| Analytics analysis | Identifying behavioural patterns |
| Competitive research | Understanding market experiences |

Do not select a method simply because it is popular. Explain why it is appropriate.

## 3. User interview framework

Structure interviews around:

1. Introduction and consent
2. Background and context
3. Current behaviour
4. Challenges and frustrations
5. Existing alternatives
6. Expectations and motivations
7. Closing questions

### Interview question principles

- Ask open-ended questions.
- Avoid leading questions.
- Ask about actual past behaviour.
- Avoid assuming users understand product terminology.
- Explore why a behaviour happens.
- Use follow-up questions to understand context.

Example:

Instead of asking:

"Would you use an automated loan application?"

Ask:

"Tell me about the last time you applied for a loan. What was difficult?"

## 4. Research synthesis

Organise findings into:

- Observed behaviour
- User statements
- Repeated patterns
- Pain points
- Motivations
- Unmet needs
- Opportunities

Separate direct evidence from interpretation.

### Insight format

**Observation:** What happened?

**Evidence:** What supports the observation?

**Interpretation:** What might explain the behaviour?

**User need:** What does the user need?

**Opportunity:** What could the product improve?

## 5. Prioritise research insights

Evaluate opportunities using:

- User impact
- Frequency
- Business relevance
- Severity
- Confidence in evidence

Do not invent research frequency, percentages, or participant quotes.

When evidence is insufficient, label the insight as a hypothesis.

## 6. Research deliverables

Choose deliverables based on the task:

- Research brief
- Participant recruitment criteria
- Interview guide
- Survey questionnaire
- Usability testing plan
- Research synthesis
- Opportunity map
- Evidence-based recommendations

## 7. Quality checklist

Before presenting research output, verify:

- Is the research objective clear?
- Are participants relevant?
- Are questions unbiased?
- Are findings supported by evidence?
- Are assumptions labelled?
- Are insights actionable?
- Are privacy and consent considered?

## Important principle

Research is not a justification exercise for a predetermined solution. It should help teams understand users and make better product decisions.
