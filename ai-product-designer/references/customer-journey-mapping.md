# Customer Journey Mapping Framework

## Purpose

Provide a structured methodology for understanding and improving end-to-end customer experiences across digital products, assisted channels, and service interactions.

## 1. Define journey scope

Before creating a journey map, establish:

- Target customer segment
- Customer goal
- Journey start point
- Journey end point
- Product or service context
- Digital and offline touchpoints
- Business objective

Avoid mapping multiple unrelated customer segments into one journey.

## 2. Journey stages

Break the experience into meaningful stages.

A typical journey may include:

1. Awareness
2. Consideration
3. Discovery
4. Onboarding
5. Verification
6. Core task completion
7. Post-completion support
8. Retention or repeat usage

Adapt stages to the actual product.

## 3. Journey mapping dimensions

For each stage, document:

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| User goal | What is the customer trying to achieve? |
| User action | What does the customer do? |
| Touchpoint | Where does the interaction happen? |
| User thought | What might the customer be thinking? |
| Emotion | What emotional experience is involved? |
| Pain point | What creates friction? |
| Opportunity | What can be improved? |
| Business impact | Why does the issue matter? |
| Evidence | What supports the finding? |

Do not invent customer thoughts or emotions. Label unvalidated interpretations as hypotheses.

## 4. Identify friction

Evaluate each stage for:

- Excessive steps
- Repeated information
- Unclear requirements
- Waiting time
- Manual intervention
- Missing feedback
- Error handling
- Channel switching
- Trust concerns
- Accessibility barriers

## 5. Opportunity identification

Translate each pain point into an opportunity.

Example:

**Pain point:** Customers do not understand why their identity verification failed.

**Opportunity:** Provide a clear explanation, specific corrective action, and a way to retry verification.

## 6. Future-state journey

For each proposed improvement, explain:

- What changes?
- What user problem does it solve?
- Which touchpoint is affected?
- What happens in the ideal experience?
- What happens when the flow fails?
- What operational or technical dependency exists?

Consider both customer-facing and employee-facing experiences when designing assisted journeys.

## 7. Journey metrics

Select metrics relevant to each stage:

- Stage completion rate
- Drop-off rate
- Time to completion
- Error rate
- Repeat attempts
- Support contact rate
- Customer satisfaction
- Conversion rate

Do not invent baselines or claim improvements without measurement.

## 8. Deliverable format

A complete journey output should contain:

1. Journey scope
2. Customer segment
3. Journey stages
4. User actions
5. Touchpoints
6. Pain points
7. Evidence and assumptions
8. Opportunities
9. Future-state recommendations
10. Success metrics

## Quality checklist

- Is the journey grounded in a defined user segment?
- Are all important stages included?
- Are offline and assisted interactions considered where relevant?
- Are pain points supported by evidence?
- Are opportunities actionable?
- Are failure and recovery journeys included?
- Are metrics measurable?

## Guiding principle

Design the complete experience around the customer's goal, not merely the sequence of screens in an application.
